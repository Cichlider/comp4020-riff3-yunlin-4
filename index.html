Why this is legal, and why it works

Courts require districts to hold roughly equal population, so all three presets above keep exactly ten voters in every district, the way a real map has to. The sandbox above won't stop you from painting a lopsided one anyway — watch the "Voters" column drift if you do, and notice that an unequal district is its own kind of gerrymander. Equal population still leaves the shape almost entirely up to whoever draws the map, and that one degree of freedom is enough. Cracking spreads a bloc thin across several districts so it's outvoted in every one. Packing does the opposite: it crowds a bloc into a small number of districts it wins by a landslide, wasting every vote past the bare majority it needed. Both are visible above: cracking takes B from 40% of the vote to zero seats; packing leaves B with one safe seat and nothing else, worse than the compact map's roughly proportional 3–2. No voter changed their mind in any of the three — only the lines did.
